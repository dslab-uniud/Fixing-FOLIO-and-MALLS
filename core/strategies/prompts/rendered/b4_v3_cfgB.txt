### Approach

### Structured Checklist
Evaluate φ̂  against the following criteria in sequence. For each, state Pass / Fail / Uncertain and provide a one-sentence justification.

  Step 0 — Semantic decomposition (pre-step): identify the entities, predicates, quantifiers, and connectives that p asserts before examining φ̂.
  Check 1 — Syntactic well-formedness: Is φ̂ a syntactically legal FOL formula? (Balanced parentheses, correct predicate arity, all variables bound, no nested relational symbols.)
  Check 2 — Ontological faithfulness: Do all predicates and constants in φ̂ appear in the provided ontology with the correct arity? Do they appear in the formula with the intended meaning? (No hallucinated predicates.)
  Check 3 — Truth-Conditional Equivalence: Does the quantifier structure of φ̂  match the most natural and defensible reading of p? (∀/∃ choice; nesting order relative to conditions).  Do, in general, p and φ̂ describe exactly the same set of situations (same models across all possible domains)? Evaluate also the completeness: Does φ̂ capture every semantic condition explicit in p, without adding or removing content?
  Check 4 — Ambiguity Resolution: Does p have different meanings? Are they all equivalent to what expressed in φ̂ ?

### Aggregation
Exploiting the information above, and maybe other remarks that are not encompassed by the above checklist, aggregate the scores above to obtain a final evaluation.

Assign a quality verdict q ∈ {'yes', '?', 'no'}:
  'yes' — φ̂ correctly and completely captures the semantic meaning of p within the provided ontology as a valid FOL formula. Note that the chosen formalism and ontology restrictions limit expressiveness: there will be cases in which not all the information in the NL sentence can be retained when converting it into a FOL formula using the given symbols. In such cases, the formalization should nonetheless be considered correct if φ̂ is the best possible approximation of p in FOL given the provided ontology.
  'no'  — φ̂  is incorrect or incomplete due to syntax errors, semantic hallucination, ontology mismatch, or logical flaws.  The only symbols permitted are the logical ones (∀ ∃ ¬ ∧ ∨ → ↔ ⊕ = ≠ True False T ⊥), and those explicitly defined in the provided ontology.
  '?'   — A confident and definitive verdict cannot be assigned because the NL sentence admits multiple non-equivalent interpretations, or because there is uncertainty about its logical correctness, or for other reasons. In this case, your comment MUST begin with exactly one of the following tags: [AMBIGUITY], [UNCERTAINTY], or [OTHER].

### Refinement
When q is "no" or "?", produce a corrected formalization ψ that resolves the specific issue identified in your analysis steps (it is a better formalization if φ̂  is considered incorrect, or it is a formalization of a different interpretation of p if it is ambiguous). ψ must be a complete, well-formed FOL formula — not a partial suggestion. Address the root cause identified in your step-by-step analysis, not just the symptom. If q is "yes", put "null" for ψ.
